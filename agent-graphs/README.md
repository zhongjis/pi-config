# Agent-graph portfolio contracts

[AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) owns portfolio-wide requirements. These graph-specific contracts govern the saved graphs alongside their schemas and prompts.

## context-gather

Saved graph: [context-gather.graph.json](context-gather.graph.json).

- `context-gather` accepts `{ request, requiredCoverage }`, plans 1–6 non-overlapping atomic-evidence tasks, and may run one evaluator-authored gap-closing iteration before synthesis. Graph-owned bounds allow at most 2 iterations, 6 tasks per iteration, and 9 total tasks. Its evaluator judges state and transition: continue only for one named material gap with distinct source/access, expected information, and no repeated or broader work; otherwise preserve partial gaps. Synthesis MUST declare partial when material required coverage remains missing.
- `context-gather` hands identity to workers through items: plan tasks use `taskId` `t1`–`t6` and evaluator gap tasks `g1`–`g6` (collision-free only while `maxIterations` ≤ 2); worker `claimId`s follow `<taskId>-c<n>`. Schema patterns enforce the format; the prompts carry the rest (claim prefixes, criterion subsets, unique task IDs). Workers do not echo `source`; the runtime-carried item identifies it. No code checks coverage IDs or claim linkage; these are prompt contracts that do not verify source truth.

## deep-research

Saved graph: [deep-research.graph.json](deep-research.graph.json); planning subgraph: [deep-research/plan.graph.json](deep-research/plan.graph.json). Design record: [ADR 0008](../docs/adr/0008-graph-agnostic-runtime-and-caller-approved-research-plan.md).

- `deep-research` accepts a nonblank `{ question, context? }`; the Caller writes purpose, audience and constraints into `context`. Its `planning` node runs the `deep-research/plan` subgraph: `seedQueries` (Wenchang) writes 2–4 broad seed queries, adding a date-bounded one only when the question asks for an origin, first use or history; `seedSearch` fans them out; `plan` (Wenchang) writes 1–6 material parts (`p1`–`p6`), each with a `doneWhen` completion bar, and 1–6 tasks that list the `partIds` they serve. A part derived from other parts says so in its `doneWhen` and gets no task.
- The `approval` decision gate declares `escalate: "always"`: every plan goes to the Caller, who approves it or rejects it with a note. A rejection re-runs `plan` once with the note appended to its prompt; a replan the Caller also rejects proceeds with `approved: false`, and the writer says so. The approved parts and bars are the contract for the rest of the run.
- Research dispatches local to Chengfeng and external GitHub/public web to Wenchang. Bounded feedback allows at most 3 rounds, 30 tasks, USD 2 of reported child cost (`spendLimit`), and 15 minutes (`deadline`); a round asking for more tasks than remain runs the first ones that fit. `spendLimit` needs every child to report cost; a child without cost ends research after that round.
- The judge asks one bool question — does the evidence meet every approved part's `doneWhen` — with `minConfidence: 0`, so a low-confidence answer never escalates (an exhausted decision chain still does). The evaluator (Wenchang) writes tasks only for gaps that block a bar, most important first, never repeats a task already in the items, and names a topic outside every approved part `outside-<slug>` with no task. `tasks: []` stops research as "no accessible route".
- Workers record the publication date a source shows, or `undated` after checking the page's own metadata, and label each claim `sourceKind` (`primary` for the authoritative record, `secondary` for reporting or commentary). They also return claims that limit, qualify or contradict the task's premise.
- Synthesis runs on Cangjie under an evidence-only contract in its node prompt: it reads only the supplied seed, research items (`r<iteration>-<index>`), gaps and stop reason, and cites each factual sentence as a link from the item id to the claim's reference. The report is the `$report` output, delivered as a Markdown file; other outputs are the plan `parts`, `approved`, per-part `coverage` (`met`/`partial`/`missing`), research `gaps`, and the declared outcome. The synthesize schema ties the outcome to coverage. No code checks that citations exist in the evidence.
